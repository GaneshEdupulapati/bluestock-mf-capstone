# Stock Market Fundamentals

## 1. What is a Stock Market?

The stock market is a marketplace where investors buy and sell shares and other securities. Companies can raise money from investors by issuing securities, while investors can buy and sell those securities.

The market has two main parts:

- **Primary Market:** New securities are issued by companies to investors.
- **Secondary Market:** Existing securities are bought and sold between investors.

## 2. NSE and BSE

**NSE (National Stock Exchange):** One of India's major stock exchanges.

**BSE (BSE Ltd.):** One of India's major stock exchanges and one of the oldest exchanges in India.

Both provide platforms for buying and selling securities.

## 3. Nifty and Sensex

**Nifty 50:** A major stock-market index associated with the NSE that represents 50 large and liquid companies.

**Sensex:** A major stock-market index associated with the BSE that represents 30 major companies.

Stock-market indices help investors and analysts understand the overall direction of the market.

## 4. IPO and SME IPO

**IPO (Initial Public Offering):** The process through which a company offers its shares to the public for the first time.

**SME IPO:** An IPO for an eligible Small and Medium Enterprise under the applicable SME exchange framework.

## 5. Market Capitalization

Market capitalization represents the market value of a company's outstanding shares.

**Formula:**

Market Capitalization = Current Share Price × Number of Outstanding Shares

Companies can generally be classified as large-cap, mid-cap, or small-cap based on their market capitalization.

## 6. P/E Ratio

P/E means Price-to-Earnings ratio.

**Formula:**

P/E = Market Price per Share / Earnings per Share

It shows how much investors are paying for each unit of a company's earnings.

## 7. P/B Ratio

P/B means Price-to-Book ratio.

**Formula:**

P/B = Market Price per Share / Book Value per Share

It compares the market value of a company's equity with its accounting book value.

## 8. EPS

EPS means Earnings Per Share.

**Formula:**

EPS = Profit Available to Equity Shareholders / Number of Outstanding Shares

EPS indicates the earnings attributable to each equity share.

## 9. Dividend

A dividend is a distribution made by a company to its shareholders from profits or eligible reserves, subject to applicable rules and company decisions.

## 10. Bonus and Stock Split

**Bonus Shares:** Additional shares issued to existing shareholders according to a specified ratio, generally without an additional payment by the shareholder.

**Stock Split:** Existing shares are divided into a larger number of shares with a proportionately lower face value.

A stock split does not by itself increase the total value of an investor's holding immediately after the split.

## 11. Trading Volume

Trading volume is the number of shares or securities traded during a particular period.

It is an indicator of trading activity in a security or market.

## 12. Company Financial Statements

### Balance Sheet

The balance sheet shows a company's financial position at a particular point in time.

**Accounting equation:**

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

### Profit and Loss Statement

The Profit and Loss statement shows a company's income and expenses during a specific period.

**Basic relationship:**

Revenue - Expenses = Profit or Loss

### Cash Flow Statement

The cash flow statement shows movements of cash and cash equivalents.

It generally contains:

- Operating activities
- Investing activities
- Financing activities

## 13. Basic Financial Ratios

Some important financial ratios are:

- **P/E Ratio:** Compares market price with earnings.
- **P/B Ratio:** Compares market value with book value.
- **EPS:** Measures earnings attributable to each share.
- **Debt-to-Equity Ratio:** Compares debt with shareholders' equity.
- **ROE:** Measures the return generated on shareholders' equity.
- **Current Ratio:** Indicates a company's short-term liquidity position.

## Conclusion

Understanding stock-market fundamentals helps Data Analysts interpret financial datasets, company financial statements, business reports, and market trends correctly. This knowledge is particularly useful when working with financial and FinTech data.
